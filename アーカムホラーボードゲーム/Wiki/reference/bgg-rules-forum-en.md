## High-confidence rulings

### Clues, trade, and the Mysterious Photo

- A clue received through a Trade action is exchanged, not gained. Therefore an effect that says “after you gain a clue,” such as Wendy Adams’s Mysterious Photo, does not trigger merely because another investigator gives you a clue.
- A genuine gain of multiple clues can produce multiple qualifying gain events when the effect and timing allow it. The BGG discussion also corrects a common misconception: there is no general rule that an item may be used only once per round.
- Clues are tradeable assets, but they are not item cards or cards in the investigator’s starting possessions. A phrase such as “starting possessions,” “cards you have,” or “items” does not include clues unless the effect says so.
- The official rule distinguishes researching a clue from performing a Research action. Researching moves a clue from an investigator’s area to the scenario sheet; a Research action includes an Observation test and may research one clue per success.

Official references: Rules Reference 412.6, 412.10, 470, and 471; Trade Action 492.2–492.4.

BGG sources: [Wendy Adams - Mysterious Photo and gaining clues](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2114448/wendy-adams-mysterious-photo-and-gaining-clues), [Trade action](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2091883/trade-action), [Trade action direction](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3516687/trade-action-direction), and [Gaining a clue but not from encounter?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088796/gaining-a-clue-but-not-from-encounter).

### Focus tokens and rerolls

- While resolving a test, any focus token you have may be spent to reroll one die. It does not have to match the skill being tested.
- Clue tokens may also be spent for rerolls. Apply rerolls and other die manipulations during the test-resolution window, before the final result is determined.
- Focus limits still apply. If an effect gives a focus token beyond the limit, follow the rule or effect that explicitly permits exceeding the limit; otherwise discard down as instructed.

Official references: Rules Reference 435.7, 435.9, 436.4, and 490.4a.

BGG sources: [Spending a Focus to re-roll](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2843055/spending-a-focus-to-re-roll) and [Question about focus action](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3731510/question-about-focus-action).

### Tests and dice pools

- The initial roll uses the calculated dice pool. If the pool is less than one die, roll one die for the initial roll.
- The number of dice is not limited by the physical dice supplied with the game; use a clear substitute when necessary.
- The “minimum one die” rule is explicitly attached to the initial roll. The BGG discussion about rerolling a zero-die pool is a community interpretation and should not be extended to other effects without checking the exact wording.
- Resolve successes and failures using the normal test procedure and the text of the effect that created the test.

Official references: Rules Reference 490.3a–c and 490.4a.

BGG sources: [Not limited by 6 dice..](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2095338/not-limited-by-6-dice) and [ReRoll 0 dice](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2777546/reroll-0-dice).

### Engagement, multiple monsters, and Evade

- An investigator may be engaged with any number of monsters. While engaged, the investigator is normally limited to Attack, Evade, and Focus actions, plus actions specifically permitted while engaged.
- One Evade test handles the engaged monsters. Apply the worst applicable Evade modifier. Each success disengages and exhausts one monster of the investigator’s choice.
- If the investigator disengages from all engaged monsters, the successful Evade grants one additional action. The investigator may then use the newly available action normally, subject to the usual once-per-round restrictions.
- A monster can be engaged with at most one investigator. Trading with an investigator who is engaged is still allowed.

Official references: Rules Reference 428.5, 428.9–428.10, 429.2, and 429.4.

BGG sources: [Evade action clarification, need help!](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2783381/evade-action-clarification-need-help), [Can you only do only one attack per turn if engaged with multiple monsters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2094485/can-you-only-do-only-one-attack-per-turn-if-engage), and [Trade action](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2091883/trade-action).

### Attack, damage, and defeated monsters

- An Attack action chooses one engaged monster. Every success deals damage to that chosen monster; damage from one Attack action is not split among multiple engaged monsters.
- The attacker applies the chosen monster’s modifier. Excess damage is not automatically transferred to another monster.
- The normal action restriction means an investigator cannot perform Attack twice in the same round merely because several monsters are engaged. A separate effect may grant or permit another action, but the effect must be followed as written.
- When a monster is defeated, remove it from the map and return its card to the top of the monster deck as directed by the game’s monster rules. If it was defeated by an Attack and has the relevant relic icon, resolve the relic-token reward. A remnant reward is tied to defeating it as part of an Attack, not to every possible defeat effect.

Official references: Rules Reference 409.2, 409.5, and 409.7, plus the monster rules in 453.

BGG sources: [Spllitting damage between multiple engaged monster](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2116949/spllitting-damage-between-multiple-engaged-monster), [Can you only do only one attack per turn if engaged with multiple monsters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2094485/can-you-only-do-only-one-attack-per-turn-if-engage), and [Fighting 2 monsters!](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2153322/fighting-2-monsters).

### Trade actions and used assets

- A Trade action can exchange any number of allies, items, spells, money, clues, and remnants with one or more investigators in the active investigator’s space.
- The other investigator may be engaged; engagement does not by itself prevent the trade.
- BGG participants commonly read the wording as allowing possessions to be passed among the investigators participating in the same Trade action, including a transfer from one non-active investigator through the active investigator to another. This is a community interpretation; use the exact table situation and any scenario text if it matters.
- The official FAQ correction to 492.4 is important: an asset used by an investigator during the action phase and then traded during that same action phase cannot be used again until the start of the next action phase. This is not a blanket “one use per item per round” rule.

Official reference: Rules Reference 492.2–492.4 and the official FAQ correction to 492.4.

BGG sources: [Trade action](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2091883/trade-action), [Trade action direction](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3516687/trade-action-direction), [Using Items while engaged with a Monster](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2976053/using-items-while-engaged-with-a-monster), and [Limit of one use per item per round?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2109526/limit-of-one-use-per-item-per-round).

### Monsters: spawn, prey, and patrol

- A spawned monster is normally ready unless its spawn instruction says it is engaged or exhausted. A ready monster spawned in an investigator’s space immediately engages that investigator.
- A monster’s spawn or engagement does not by itself resolve its attack; monster attacks occur in the Monster Phase unless another effect says otherwise.
- A Patrol monster follows its destination. Its prey value helps determine whom it engages when the movement or destination puts it in a position to engage; it does not generally override the patrol destination.
- When there are equally short legal routes or destinations, the investigators choose. Hunter movement uses prey differently and should be resolved from the monster’s own movement instruction.
- Elusive monsters do not automatically engage in the usual way, though they may still be engaged by an effect that allows it.

Official references: Rules Reference 453.7, 463.2, and 467.5.

BGG sources: [Monster Spawn - Auto Engage?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088564/monster-spawn-auto-engage), [Monster movement toward destinations](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2122462/monster-movement-toward-destinations), and [Prey or Patrol? Which Takes Precedence?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2093407/prey-or-patrol-which-takes-precedence).

### Encounters, event cards, anomalies, and headlines

- When a location encounter’s clue event is resolved and the clue is gained, place that event card face up in the event discard area. If the clue is not gained, return that event card with the top two cards of the encounter deck as instructed.
- An anomaly encounter card is a non-event encounter card: after resolving it, place it on the bottom of the deck from which it was drawn. Do not apply a discard-and-reshuffle house rule unless a scenario specifically instructs it.
- If the headline deck is empty, follow the headline rule that adds doom to the scenario sheet. Do not infer a reshuffle unless the rules or scenario text says to reshuffle.
- The “research” terminology is precise: moving a clue is not automatically an Observation test.

Official references: the encounter and mythic-phase rules, plus the official FAQ clarification that an anomaly card goes to the bottom of its source deck.

BGG sources: [Headline / Outbreak / Anomaly discard pile?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3730472/headline-outbreak-anomaly-discard-pile) and [Gaining a clue but not from encounter?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088796/gaining-a-clue-but-not-from-encounter).
