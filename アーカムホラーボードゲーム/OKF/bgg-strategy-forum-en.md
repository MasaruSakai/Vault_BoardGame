# Arkham Horror Third Edition - BGG Strategy Forum Digest

This file organizes the strategy discussions listed in the BoardGameGeek [Strategy forum](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/257499/arkham-horror-third-edition/forums/67) for Arkham Horror Third Edition.

The forum contained 30 topics when reviewed. It is a community strategy forum, not an official rules source. The advice below is a synthesis of recurring ideas and should not be treated as a guaranteed optimal solution. When a strategy depends on a rule, use the [official Rules Reference](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/70/15/70156879-aefe-441e-94a0-1a816872058d/ahb01_rules-reference-compressed.pdf), the [official FAQ and errata](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/39/90/3990f6ae-b143-43a2-a1ea-8aff693addaf/ahb_faq_9-18-20-good.pdf), and the card or scenario text first.

## Core strategy

### Control the board before pushing the story

- The Action Phase is often spent keeping the board from spiraling: ward doom, defeat or manage dangerous monsters, and move to a useful position.
- In the Encounter Phase, investigators improve their position through clues, items, spells, allies, money, healing, and anomaly encounters.
- If doom and monsters are left unchecked, later actions are spent only repairing the board. Several forum posts describe this as falling behind even when the investigators are technically making progress.
- In the early game, containment and preparation are often more valuable than rushing a story objective. As investigators gain bonuses, the same action becomes more efficient later.

This is a strategic model from community discussion, not a separate game rule. Scenarios that explicitly reward immediate story progress can change the priority.

Sources: [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far), [Need tips for new players.](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088124/need-tips-for-new-players.), and [Dealing with monsters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3679469/dealing-with-monsters).

### Plan the round around action efficiency

- Discuss the plan before the Action Phase: who handles doom, who handles monsters, who needs the next encounter, and which investigator should act first.
- Resolve actions in an order that preserves options. If the best warder fails, another investigator may still attempt it; if the first investigator succeeds, the next investigator can spend actions elsewhere.
- Treat movement as a real cost. Crossing the map to solve a problem can consume most of a small team’s available actions.
- A successful Evade against all engaged monsters grants an additional action. Exhausting a monster can be better than spending several actions to defeat it immediately when the group needs to preserve momentum.
- Extra-action effects, transportation, and movement replacement effects are powerful because they reduce the main bottleneck: actions. They do not remove the normal once-per-round restrictions on action types.

Sources: [Need tips for new players.](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088124/need-tips-for-new-players.), [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far), [Witch Blood - How to Use?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2583959/witch-blood-how-to-use), and [Skids’ On the Lam](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2375392/skids-on-the-lam).

### Prepare before the board becomes dangerous

Recurring advice for the first few rounds:

- Reach the focus limit when practical.
- Acquire a weapon or offensive spell early enough that the first serious monster does not consume the team’s whole turn.
- Prioritize items or allies that grant an extra action, such as Pocket Watch or Gabriel, when the investigator and scenario can exploit them.
- Add bonuses to the skills the investigator will repeatedly use, usually attack, ward, evade, or research.
- Use damage and horror soaks to cover the investigator’s weaker side, rather than assuming every hit can be avoided.
- Keep some money available. Money can buy items during encounters and can also pay for extra movement or scenario effects.

The forum contains different opinions about the exact order. The stable principle is to improve action quality before the mythos pressure becomes too large.

Sources: [Need tips for new players.](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088124/need-tips-for-new-players.), [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far), and [Turn 1 Trades](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2094650/turn-1-trades).

### Treat encounters as the engine of growth

- Location icons are useful planning signals. A General Store or Curiosity Shoppe is a likely destination when the team needs an item; other locations are better for different asset types or abilities.
- A clue encounter often provides the location’s ordinary benefit as well as a clue. Therefore, do not automatically cross the map just to chase a clue, but do not avoid a clue space when its ordinary encounter is useful.
- Clues are also valuable as test rerolls. A failed test is not always a reason to abandon an encounter plan when a clue can rescue an important roll.
- Research clues in batches when possible. A high-Observation investigator can move several clues to the scenario sheet from one successful Research action.
- Anomaly encounters may remove doom, so resolving an anomaly can be board control rather than a distraction from the objective.

The exact result of an encounter is always determined by the card. The forum advice is about planning around likely value, not replacing the encounter deck.

Sources: [Should we avoid chasing clues early?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3130953/should-we-avoid-chasing-clues-early), [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far), and [Buying item in 3rd Edition- rather difficult](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2229577/buying-item-in-3rd-edition-rather-difficult).

## Team composition and investigator use

### Cover the essential jobs

The recurring community recommendation is to build a team that can cover:

- **Ward:** remove doom before it creates anomalies or reaches the scenario sheet.
- **Monster management:** defeat dangerous monsters, especially those that generate doom or otherwise worsen the board.
- **Investigation:** collect and research clues, and use Observation or other encounter strengths.
- **Mobility and support:** move efficiently, trade assets, heal, and provide extra actions or skill bonuses.

At least one reliable fighter and one reliable warder are repeatedly recommended. A balanced team is safer, but labels such as Guardian or Mystic are not the whole decision: individual stats, starting assets, scenario setup, codex cards, and the visible item display matter.

Sources: [General strategy against GOO (Ancient Ones)?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2792245/general-strategy-against-goo-ancient-ones), [Are Unusual Team Compositions Viable?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3455621/are-unusual-team-compositions-viable), and [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far).

### Unusual teams can work, but action efficiency matters more

- Unusual combinations are viable when the group understands how to cover the missing role with assets, trades, and encounter choices.
- A team with two Guardians or two Mystics may still succeed if the scenario’s immediate demands and the available items support it.
- The forum also notes that a fun but inefficient team can become a long, nearly unwinnable game. The important test is whether the team can keep up with the board, not whether it matches a fixed class template.
- Before choosing investigators, inspect the item display when the scenario setup allows it. A strong weapon or mobility item can make an unconventional investigator much more viable.

Source: [Are Unusual Team Compositions Viable?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3455621/are-unusual-team-compositions-viable).

### Use Trade to build the team you actually need

- The official rules and FAQ allow starting spells, allies, and items to be traded; talents and conditions normally cannot be traded.
- A first-round Trade action can redistribute starting assets among investigators in the same space. This can turn a starting weapon, spell, vehicle, ally, or money pool into a more coherent team.
- The forum repeatedly suggests moving weapons toward the investigator who will fight, spells toward the strongest caster, and mobility or extra-action assets toward the investigator responsible for warding or covering the board.
- Stella Clark’s move-and-trade ability is discussed as an action-saving way to move investigators and redistribute assets, but it requires careful coordination and is most useful when several investigators travel together.
- The official FAQ’s 492.4 correction still matters: an asset used during the Action Phase and then traded during that same phase cannot be used again until the next Action Phase.

Sources: [Turn 1 Trades](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2094650/turn-1-trades), [Stella Clark trading strategy with Charlie Kane or Jenny Barnes](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3253474/stella-clark-trading-strategy-with-charlie-kane-or), and [official FAQ 9/18/20](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/39/90/3990f6ae-b143-43a2-a1ea-8aff693addaf/ahb_faq_9-18-20-good.pdf).

## Combat and survival

### Decide whether to defeat, evade, or delay

- A fighter should not automatically attack every monster immediately. The correct choice depends on whether the monster threatens the board, whether the attack is likely to defeat it, and whether the investigator needs the encounter.
- Evading all engaged monsters can refund an action and exhaust the monsters. This is especially useful for a survivor-style investigator or for a fighter who needs to create a safe route for the rest of the team.
- A monster that is exhausted does not attack during the current Monster Phase; it normally readies at the end of that phase. Check the monster and card text for exceptions.
- Evading is much harder as a solo plan when monsters move directly to their prey or continually add doom. In a group, one investigator can evade while a specialist handles the monster later.
- Card-specific stalling tools such as Skids’ On the Lam can be strong, but they may cost an encounter or other utility. Read the card’s timing and re-engagement text carefully.

Sources: [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far), [Dealing with monsters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3679469/dealing-with-monsters), [Skids’ On the Lam](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2375392/skids-on-the-lam), and [Could I have played solo Urchin better?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2148721/could-i-have-played-solo-urchin-better).

### Do not let dangerous monsters accumulate

Several posts describe a snowball effect:

- More monsters consume movement and combat actions.
- Missed encounters reduce access to weapons, clues, money, and healing.
- Monsters with lurker or doom-related abilities can make the board worse even when they do not attack.
- A single fighter may be able to handle a normal monster, but elite or immediately engaging monsters can tie that fighter up for multiple rounds.

The practical rule of thumb is to identify which monster is actually worsening the board and spend attacks on that threat first. Do not assume every monster has equal priority.

Sources: [Dealing with monsters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3679469/dealing-with-monsters), [Monsters are relentless](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2087123/monsters-are-relentless), and [General strategy against GOO (Ancient Ones)?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2792245/general-strategy-against-goo-ancient-ones).

### Investigator defeat is not automatically required

The strategy forum contains both reports of frequent investigator deaths and successful games without any deaths. The common conclusion is:

- Defeat is not a necessary planned step in normal play.
- In a low-player-count game, losing one investigator removes a large share of the team’s action coverage and can be especially damaging.
- Some difficult scenarios may make accepting a defeat reasonable when a replacement investigator can immediately solve a critical problem, but this is a situational recovery line, not a general rule.

Source: [Do I need to kill my characters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2876640/do-i-need-to-kill-my-characters).

## Player count and solo play

### Two investigators

- Two investigators are viable, including for difficult expansion scenarios, but there is less redundancy and less ability to cover distant neighborhoods.
- Choose investigators who can perform multiple jobs, reduce movement costs, or create extra actions.
- Coordinate turns tightly and avoid sending both investigators across the map for separate minor problems.
- The forum contains conflicting personal experience about which scenarios are manageable with two investigators. Treat the recommended scenario order as a learning suggestion, not a difficulty guarantee.

Sources: [Secrets of the Order: foolish with two investigators?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3170243/secrets-of-the-order-foolish-with-two-investigator) and [Tactic/strategy guides for 2-player games?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2726756/tacticstrategy-guides-for-2-player-games).

### Solo play

- Solo play magnifies movement, action economy, and bad monster draws. One investigator can be locked out of encounters while dealing with a monster.
- A weapon or offensive spell is especially important when no other investigator can take over the fight.
- Extra actions and strong mobility are unusually valuable. The forum repeatedly points to Pocket Watch, vehicles, and early powerful items as major swing factors.
- A plan based only on evasion is fragile in solo play against monsters that move directly to their prey or generate doom.

Sources: [Could I have played solo Urchin better?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2148721/could-i-have-played-solo-urchin-better), [Need tips for new players.](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088124/need-tips-for-new-players.), and [Do I need to kill my characters?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2876640/do-i-need-to-kill-my-characters).

### Four or more investigators and unofficial variants

Some community members feel that higher player counts become harder because elite monsters and mythos pressure can scale more harshly than investigators’ extra encounters compensate for. Others point out that more investigators improve coverage and make it easier for someone to answer a new problem.

The following suggestions from the forum are unofficial variants, not part of the base rules:

- Add one blank mythos token for 4-5 investigators and a second for 6-7 investigators.
- Use Story Mode from Under Dark Waves, where applicable.
- Replace added blank tokens with doom or monster tokens for harder custom modes.

Record these as house rules if used. Do not mix them into the authoritative OKF rules.

Sources: [What’s the best number of investigators?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2291572/whats-the-best-number-of-investigators) and [What Have We Learned So Far?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2235371/what-have-we-learned-so-far).

## Scenario-specific discussion

The following notes contain spoilers and are community advice, not official scenario instructions.

### Approach of Azathoth

- Multiple players recommend not using this as the first learning scenario despite the printed recommendation.
- Ward doom early and treat anomalies as an emergency. Do not allow cultists or other doom-producing monsters to remain unchecked.
- Obtain a weapon or offensive spell and, when possible, an extra-action asset before the board becomes overwhelmed.
- Some players recommend building a clue reserve before advancing the story; others recommend focusing on the story once the board is stable. The correct balance depends on the scenario information available to the group.
- The forum reports that learning the scenario’s plot and likely locations substantially changes the difficulty. This is spoiler knowledge, not a general rule.

Sources: [How the hell do I beat Azathoth scenario?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2340222/how-the-hell-do-i-beat-azathoth-scenario), [Need tips for new players.](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2088124/need-tips-for-new-players.), and [What’s your order of easiest to hardest scenarios?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2292396/whats-your-order-of-easiest-to-hardest-scenarios).

### Veil of Twilight

- A community spoiler discussion warns that refusing to join the Lodge can add an extra monster and Gate Burst mythos token, increasing early pressure.
- If avoiding that route, high mobility or teleportation is mentioned as a way to compensate.
- Treat this as a scenario-route observation and verify the actual scenario and codex text at the table.

Source: [Spoilers: Beating the Veil of Twilight good way](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2414787/spoilers-beating-the-veil-of-twilight-good-way).

### The Dead Cry Out

- Stella Clark’s move-and-trade ability is discussed as a way to save movement actions and redistribute items, allies, money, and remnants while investigators travel together.
- This is strongest when the group plans its actions in advance and can keep the relevant investigators in the same space.

Source: [Stella Clark trading strategy with Charlie Kane or Jenny Barnes](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3253474/stella-clark-trading-strategy-with-charlie-kane-or).
